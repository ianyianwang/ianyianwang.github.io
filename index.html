📝Report card
Week 13 · All years

Results are in: I've been evaluated

  1. The report card. This time, the students did the grading. 59 students in Years 7, 8 and 9 rated me on 24 statements, from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). My overall average is 3.78 out of 5. By year: Year 7 gave me 3.86, Year 9 3.75 and Year 8 3.70. Year 7 are the generous markers. Year 8 kept me humble.
  2. What I'm good at. Lessons are well prepared (4.54). Nothing less than full effort is accepted (4.36). The teacher arrives on time (4.31). The class doesn't waste time (4.27). In short: organised, punctual and slightly relentless.
  3. What I'm bad at. Making students feel I really care about them (2.96). Students not being afraid to answer, even if they might be wrong (3.02). Enjoying the lessons (3.27). Being encouraged to share their thoughts (3.35). Message received. Students want more of the calm, friendly version of me they see outside class.
  4. Best comments. Funny: asked to finish "One thing I really like about this class is…", one student wrote: "Mr Wang loves to give homework." Heartwarming: "It's pretty fun and helps me pay attention more in class! It's one of my favourite classes too."
🥄Spoon check
Week 12 · All years

Calendar spotlight: the great spoon test

  1. One odd line. This week, the Homework + Class Calendar had one unusual item: bring a spoon to class. It wasn't on the slides or the board. The only way to know about it was to read the calendar.
  2. The results. In Year 7, 58% of the class brought a spoon (14 of 24). In Year 8 it was 37% (7 of 19), and in Year 9 it was 52% (11 of 21). Everyone who remembered got extra credit. Roy (Year 7) brought in a fork instead.
  3. The point. Checking the calendar is a habit, and good habits make Year 7, 8 and 9 much easier. Next time, the calendar might ask for something else. Keep reading it.

Week 11 · Year 7

Homework spotlight: Year 7 is on a roll

  1. Everyone handed it in. This week's homework was a set of review questions on Unit 2.3, and every Year 7 student completed it. A few weeks ago, about 8 in 10 students were handing in their homework.
  2. And it's better work, too. The class scored an average of 90% on the Unit 2.3 review questions. On the Unit 2.2 review questions a few weeks ago, the average was 63%. This time, 15 students got every question right.
  3. Reading is up too. ReadTheory is our online reading practice. In Week 9, 14 students did their reading quizzes. In Week 10, every student did, and 22 of 24 finished all four quizzes.
Week 11 · Year 9

Project spotlight: Year 9 meets the Purple Community Fund

  1. Year 9 kicked off Design Thinking Project 2. Their client this time is the Purple Community Fund (PCF), a charity that supports children and families in the Philippines.
  2. Six groups, three real challenges: corporate gifts made from ring pulls, eco-fashion and festival wear, and a catalogue of learning videos for the children at PCF, most of whom don't speak English.
  3. First stop: empathy. Who is the user, what do they need, and what bugs them about what's out there now? After that: ideas, prototypes, testing and a group pitch.

👻Halloween reads
Week 9 · All years

Reading spotlight: horror stories for Halloween week

  1. Year 7: The Monkey's Paw by W. W. Jacobs (1902). A dried monkey's paw that grants three wishes. What could go wrong? Before the final part, the class argued it out: is the paw really magic, or is it all coincidence?
  2. Year 8: Small Horrors by Darcy Coates. Year 8 read the first story in the collection, "The Dog's Grave Digger". It has an O. Henry-style twist at the end. No spoilers here.
  3. Year 9: The Lottery by Shirley Jackson (1948). A friendly village, a sunny June morning and a lottery nobody wants to win. When it was first published, many readers wrote in angry and confused.
