The sounds

Your child learns the sounds in three sets. Set 1 comes first. Most children start Set 2 and Set 3 when they can read Set 1 sounds quickly.

The most important tip: say each sound short and pure. Say “mmm”, not “muh”. Say “t”, not “tuh”. Children who hear pure sounds find it much easier to blend words.

Set 1 sounds

One letter, one sound (and the first “special friends”: two letters that make one sound).

masdt inpgo ckub felhsh rjvyw thzchquxngnk

Stretchy sounds: you can hold them (“sssss”)Bouncy sounds: short and quick (“b”)

Set 2 sounds

Long vowel sounds. Each one is made by “special friends”.

ayeeighowoooo arorairirouoy

Why is oo there twice? Because it makes two different sounds: long oo in “zoo” and short oo in “look”.

Set 3 sounds

New ways to write the sounds your child already knows. For example, ay (play), a-e (make) and ai (rain) all make the same sound.

eaoia-ei-eo-eu-e awareurerowai oaewireearuretion tious / ciouse
