SBS Celebrates Its Second Loy Krathong Festival

By Oscar Wood, Year 5Tuesday 24 November 2026

SBS Bangkok held its second Loy Krathong festival on Tuesday 24 November, with food stalls, games and a raffle.

Many students dressed up in Thai traditional clothing called chud Thai.

The food stalls were so popular that they ran out of food. There were also games, such as ring toss and tin can alley, and a raffle where you could win plushies and other toys.

Tuesday’s atmosphere was filled with excitement. There were very long lines, and people were shouting and cheering.

Loy Krathong Celebration (SBS Bangkok) 2026

By Pordee Panyagometh, Year 6Tuesday 24 November 2026

On November 24, 2026 SBS Bangkok held its second Loy Krathong festival.

Many parents and children have come to attend the festival. At the festival there were many games. Many people played the games and watched the performances that the students had prepared from Toddlers up to Year 9.

A few parents and students were interviewed. “I really enjoyed the performances, especially the little dek dek’s dances,” said one parent. “I enjoyed the games,” said a student. Everyone interviewed enjoyed the performances and the games. There were also many snacks like pop corn, ice cream, and cotton candy.

Here are some fun facts about Loy Krathong. Loy Krathong has been celebrated since the 13th or 14th century. People come to Thailand every year to see the festival with lanterns and krathongs.

Reducing water pollution is a problem that comes up during Loy krathong. To help, there are digital loy krathongs people can make online. Some people also make Krathongs out of natural materials: banana stalks, banana leaves, betel nut sheaths, corn husks, coconut shells, lotus flowers.
